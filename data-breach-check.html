How to check for a data breach

It takes about ten seconds.

  1. Type your email address in the box above.
  2. Read the list of breaches that contain it. Each one shows the date and what was exposed.
  3. Follow the steps further down this page for the kinds of data that were exposed.

Check every address you use: work, personal, and the old one you still log in with.

Breach is the company or service the data was taken from. It is often a site you signed up to years ago.

Data exposed lists what was in the stolen file next to your email. This is the line that decides what you do next.

Password risk tells you how the passwords were stored. "Plain text" means anyone with the file can read them. "Easy to crack" means they were scrambled weakly. "Hard to crack" means they were scrambled well, which buys you time but is not a reason to keep the password. "Unknown" means we could not confirm how they were stored, so treat the password as exposed.

Have I been hacked? How to read the signs

Being in a breach and being hacked are not the same thing. A breach means your details were in a file stolen from a company. Being hacked means someone got into one of your accounts.

A breach makes a hack more likely, mostly when you reused the password somewhere else.

Signs that someone is in your account:

  • Password reset emails you did not ask for.
  • Login alerts from a place or device you do not know.
  • Messages or posts you did not send.
  • Charges or orders you do not recognise.
  • You are locked out, and your password no longer works.

If you see any of these, change that password now from a device you trust, and turn on two-step login.

How our data is sourced and verified

Every breach is checked before it goes in. It needs either a statement from the affected company or coverage by the press, and each entry carries its reference. Today 756 of the 790 breaches carry the Verified tag.

We keep email addresses and the public facts about each breach. We never keep the stolen passwords in readable form, card numbers, ID documents or other sensitive data.

The whole process is written up on our methodology page, and the code is open source.

Data breach check: questions and answers

How do I check for a data breach?

Type your email address into the box at the top of this page. You will see every breach in our index that contains it, with the date and the kinds of data exposed. It is free and needs no account.

Have I been hacked?

Not necessarily. Being in a breach means your details were in a file stolen from a company, not that someone is in your account. Check for the signs listed above, and change any password you reused.

Is this data breach check free?

Yes. The check is free, with no account and no payment. XposedOrNot is open source and has run as a free service since 2017.

How many breaches do you check?

790 breaches holding 11.7 billion records, and the list grows as new breaches are verified. The ten newest are shown on this page.

What should I do if my email is in a breach?

Look at what was exposed, then act on that. If passwords were exposed, change that password everywhere you used it and turn on two-step login. The steps for each kind of data are listed above.

How is this different from a password check?

This page checks an email address and shows which breaches contain it. The password leak check tests a password on its own, without your email, to see if it appears in known leaks. Use both.
